# Sources — 2026-06-22-field-fortifications-system

## Primary (Tier 1)

1. **Wikipedia "Dragon's teeth (fortification)"** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon%27s_teeth_(fortification)
   Canonical reference: pyramidal anti-tank obstacles of reinforced concrete, 90-120 cm tall, first used WWII.
   Siegfried Line / Atlantic Wall: concrete mat 10-30 m wide, sunk 1-2 m, staggered rows. Reused in
   Ukraine 2022-2026 (Surovikin Line).

2. **Wikipedia "Czech hedgehog"** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_hedgehog
   Anti-tank obstacle of 3 × L140/I-beam arms (1.8-2.1 m, 198-240 kg), forms octahedron axes. Invented
   by Major František Kašík, Czechoslovakia 1938. Effective even tipped over. 1200 pieces/km of front.

3. **Wikipedia "Barbed wire"** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbed_wire
   Belts 15 m deep, WWI screw pickets for silent night installation, Bangalore torpedo breaching.
   1 platoon × 1 km/hr concertina deployment (from Wikipedia "Concertina wire").

4. **Wikipedia "Concertina wire"** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concertina_wire
   Triple concertina: 5 men × 50 yards / 15 min. Modern variant "Yegoza" used extensively in Ukraine:
   16,000 km constructed per Ukrainian State Special Transport Service (2025).

5. **Wikipedia "Hesco bastion"** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesco_bastion
   Collapsible wire mesh + geotextile gabion, fill with sand/soil/gravel. 1989 invention, modular,
   stackable. ~1000 sandbags/hour equivalent labor.

6. **Wikipedia "Field fortification"** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_fortification
   Temporary vs permanent classification. Field fortification = my prototype domain.

7. **Wikipedia "Anti-tank ditch"** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-tank_ditch
   Width 9-12 ft, depth 6-8 ft, zigzag course. 340 km constructed in Ukraine (2025).
   Combined with dragon's teeth + barbed wire as layered system.

8. **Wikipedia "Czechoslovak border fortifications"** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovak_border_fortifications
   First concrete hedgehogs (1935-1936), replaced with metal due to machine-gun vulnerability.
